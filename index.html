It began on Lake Maracaibo. Today most of Venezuela's heavy crude lies in the far east, while much of its refining sits on the western coast. Moving oil across that distance takes pipelines, upgraders, and a great deal of electricity from a grid that often fails. This project maps that journey and asks whether renewables could keep it running.

Oil needs power

As operations recovered, electricity demand from the national oil industry rose by roughly a fifth.

A solar proposal

Maracaibo calls itself "La Tierra del Sol Amada". The proposal is to take advantage of that sun for the oil fields.

  • Solar and storage for oilfield and upgrader loads.
  • Flared gas as a bridge, not a destination.
  • Homes and hospitals kept off the same strained lines.

Wind for Falcón

Falcón is known for its wind. The refineries of Paraguaná sit on one of Venezuela's windiest coasts.

  • Wind power for the Paraguaná refining complex.
  • Sun and wind together, so the supply is steadier than either alone.
