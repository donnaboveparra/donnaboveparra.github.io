Sources

Data

All tourism figures come from Eurostat’s accommodation statistics. I downloaded each dataset in full from the Eurostat dissemination API (the base URL plus the dataset code and the format, here as a compressed TSV file) and kept only Italy’s rows. The regional datasets are: Nights spent at tourist accommodation establishments by NUTS 2 regions (tour_occ_nin2); Arrivals at tourist accommodation establishments by NUTS 2 regions (tour_occ_arn2); Nights spent by month and NUTS 2 regions (tour_occ_nin2m); Establishments, bedrooms and bed places by NUTS 2 regions (tour_cap_nuts2); and Population on 1 January by age, sex and NUTS 2 region (demo_r_d2jan).

For the province view, I added the following indicators: Nights spent at tourist accommodation establishments by NUTS 3 regions (tour_occ_nin3) and Population on 1 January by age group, sex and NUTS 3 region (demo_r_pjangrp3). Monthly figures are published at region level only, so the province tooltip shows the busiest months of the parent region. At province level, the map offers only the indicators that can be computed from these files.

Definitions

A night spent is each night a guest actually sleeps in a tourist accommodation establishment (see Eurostat’s reference metadata). The figures cover hotels, holiday and other short-stay accommodation, and campsites. Tourism intensity is calculated as nights divided by the resident population on 1 January. Foreign share is nights spent by non-residents divided by all nights. Bed places are counted per 1,000 residents. Seasonality is the share of a year’s nights that fall in the two busiest months (available from 2020). Average stay is nights divided by arrivals. For more on regional tourism, see Eurostat’s article on tourism statistics at regional level.

Boundaries and tools

Region and province boundaries are Eurostat GISCO’s NUTS 2021 files at 1:1 million scale (regions, provinces), © EuroGeographics. I cleaned the data in Python through Colab with pandas and GeoPandas, and drew the map with D3.js.
