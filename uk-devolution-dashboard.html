Alcohol-specific deaths in six UK cities, compared with London

← Back to the portfolio

Did devolved alcohol policy show up in the numbers?

Health is a devolved power. Scotland introduced a minimum price per unit of alcohol on 1 May 2018, and Wales followed on 2 March 2020. Northern Ireland has not introduced one: in March 2026 its Health Minister said the legislation could not be delivered before the next Assembly election. England has not either, so London is the comparison line on every chart.

How to read the charts

  • Each line is an index: the 3-year rolling average of alcohol-specific deaths, where 2018 = 100. A value of 120 means 20% higher than in 2018.
  • The solid line is the city and the dashed grey line is London.
  • The red shaded area marks the years minimum unit pricing has been in force. Belfast and Derry have none.
  • Hover over a year to see the actual number of deaths registered that year.

What stands out

By 2024 London’s index was about 150, well above all six cities: Cardiff 135, Derry 123, Swansea 111, Belfast 109, Glasgow 104 and Edinburgh 101. Cardiff was already 36% above its 2018 level in 2020, the year Wales introduced the policy, so the charts do not show a simple before-and-after pattern.

Limitations

  • Counts, not rates. The index follows the number of deaths and does not adjust for population.
  • Small numbers. Cardiff, Swansea and Derry have only a few dozen deaths a year, so a difference of five or ten moves the line a lot.
  • Year of registration. Deaths are counted when registered, not when they happened.
  • Residents. Scotland’s and Northern Ireland’s figures include non-residents; the ONS figures for Wales and London do not.
  • Areas are not the same as cities. Derry’s area includes Strabane, and London is a whole region.
  • Correlation, not cause. Other things changed at the same time, including COVID-19 from 2020, so these charts cannot show that the policy caused any change.

Data and code

Sources: National Records of Scotland, Office for National Statistics and NISRA. The data was cleaned in Python and saved as six chart files (JSON) on GitHub. A loop in this page embeds each one.
